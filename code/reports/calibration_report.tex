\vspace{-0.6cm}

\noindent\small
The model is a small open economy with overlapping generations, solved in
detrended per-capita units. A hat denotes $\hat x_t=X_t/(Z_tN_t)$, where
$Z_t=(1+g)^t$ is labour productivity and $N_t$ population, so stocks
accumulate at the gross rate $\Gamma_t=(1+g)(1+n_t)$. On the balanced growth
path $\hat x$ is constant, levels grow at $\Gamma-1$ and per-capita
quantities at $g$. Preferences are $\log c-\nu\ell^{1+\varphi}/(1+\varphi)$.
Households hold domestic capital and foreign assets, both earning $r$.
Government debt is held abroad by official creditors at the rate $r_B$. TFP
$A$ is set so that $\hat y=1$ in 2023, so ratios to output can be read as
levels. The period is a year.

\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\footnotesize
Sources for the externally set parameters that are not read directly from a
table: $K_g/Y$ is the IMF ICSD public capital stock for 2019, carried forward
to 2023 by perpetual inventory with national-accounts public investment and
real GDP. $\delta_g$ is estimated from the ICSD stock over 2000--2019; it is
within 0.001 of $I_g/K_g-(\Gamma_0-1)$. $r_B$ is the implicit interest rate on
the debt stock, averaged over 2012--24. $b_{min}$ is the Greek national pension,
\EUR{413.76} a month from January 2023, divided by output per person aged
25--84: \EUR{224.7}bn of GDP (Eurostat) over 7{,}559{,}564 people, or
\EUR{29{,}722} each. The pension amount comes from the legislation and the
2024 Ageing Report country fiche, since Eurostat reports only aggregate pension
spending. The statutory retirement age is 67 in 2023. Under Law 4336/2015 it
moves one for one with life expectancy at 65, reviewed every three years; the
model applies the reviews from 2024, with life expectancy the mean of men and
women in the EUROPOP2023 baseline projection. Each cohort retires at the
statutory age in force when it reaches it, rounded to a whole year: cohorts
entering in 2023 retire at 72, and those entering from 2050 on at 74.

$\tau^p$ and $\rho^{pens}$ also have direct counterparts in the data. They
differ because the model applies $\tau^p$ to the whole wage bill, with no
contribution ceiling, and $\rho^{pens}$ to career-average earnings capacity

wage.

\section*{Demographics}
\vspace{-0.1cm}

\noindent\footnotesize
The calibration uses the measured 2023 age distribution, not a stationary
population.\footnote{A stationary population with the same dependency ratio
would miss the age profile, in particular the large cohorts about to retire
that drive the projected rise in the ratio.} Each cohort faces the survival
rates of its own birth year. After 2023, survival and the size of entering
cohorts follow the EUROPOP2023 baseline projection to 2100, in the baseline and
in every policy experiment. After 2100, mortality stays at its 2100 level and
the growth of entering cohorts falls to zero by 2120. The age distribution is
constant from 2179 on.

Since the 2023 population is not stationary, the economy is in transition
even without any policy change. Prices do not move: the return is set abroad
and the wage follows from it. Ageing changes household decisions only through
the retirement age, which is higher for later cohorts. Households also respond
once a tax adjusts to finance the budget, as in the tax-financed experiments.

carries about 20 times the stock's simulation noise. Household wealth is
$A=K^{dom}+NFA$. All movement comes from the age structure and the rising
retirement age; the steps fall in the years when successive cohorts' retirement
age rises by one year.}
