\noindent\small
The model is solved in per-capita detrended units: aggregates are divided by
$Z_tN_t$ with $Z_t=(1+g)^t$, so every stock recursion carries
$\Gamma=(1+g)(1+n)$ and the household budget carries $(1+g)$ on next-period
assets. Preferences are $\log c-\nu\ell^{1+\varphi}/(1+\varphi)$. Government
debt is external and official-sector: households hold domestic capital and
foreign assets at $r$; the government pays $r_B$ abroad. A hat denotes a
detrended per-capita quantity $\hat x_t=X_t/(Z_tN_t)$, constant along the
balanced growth path while levels grow at $\Gamma-1$ and per-capita terms at
$g$; $A$ is set so $\hat y=1$, which makes ratios to output readable as
levels. Parameters are annual.

\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\footnotesize
Data sources for the externally set block, where the mapping is not a direct
read: $K_g/Y$ is the IMF ICSD stock of 2019 rolled to 2023 by perpetual

\section*{Demographics}
\vspace{-0.1cm}

\noindent\footnotesize
The base year is 2023 and its age distribution enters as
\emph{measured}, not as a stationary population, so the calibration matches
2023. Model ages $0$--$59$ are real ages $25$--$84$. Each age is weighted by
its cohort's size at birth, mortality being already reflected in the simulated
averages in which the dead hold zero; the cross-section counts the living, so
each age is divided by its cumulative survival probability to recover those
weights. Survival and entering cohorts then follow the EUROPOP2023 baseline
projection through 2100, identically across the baseline and every
counterfactual. Past it mortality is held at the 2100 schedule and the growth
rate of entering cohorts is taken to zero by 2120, so one lifespan later, from
2179, the age distribution is exactly constant.\footnote{A stationary population matched on the dependency ratio
would reproduce that ratio but not the age shape, lacking the cohort about to
retire that drives the projected rise.} Because the base-year population is not stationary the economy is in
transition from $t=0$ with no policy change: detrended aggregates move while
the older cohorts retire, settling once the demographic transition completes.
The balanced-growth check in Table~5 is computed over the periods after that,
the last 24 of the horizon. With tax rates fixed the age structure enters no household's
problem --- the return is exogenous and the wage is pinned by the firm's
first-order condition --- so ageing moves aggregates and the government budget
alone. It reaches households once financing responds: in the tax-financed
experiments ageing raises pension and health spending, the labour income tax
rises to meet the debt target, and decisions move with it.

\noindent\footnotesize
The detrended trend is the mean log growth per period,
$\frac{1}{T-t_0}\sum_{t\ge t_0}\log(\hat x_{t+1}/\hat x_t)$, over the periods
from $t_0$, the first on the balanced growth path. It is zero under correct
detrending and would read $g$ or $\Gamma_T-1$ for a variable that was left in
levels. The earlier periods are excluded because the age distribution is still
changing: entering cohorts follow the projection and each cohort faces the
mortality of its own birth year, so neither the population nor its composition
is constant. The noise floor is the same statistic on a run with $g=0$ and a
constant population, where the true value is also zero; only departures
materially above it are informative.
